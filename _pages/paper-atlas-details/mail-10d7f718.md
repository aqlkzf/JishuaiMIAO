## MAIL：树形多智能体系统中的在线激励学习

### 论文要解决什么问题？

论文研究一棵“层层委托”的树：每个节点既是上级的代理人，又是下级的委托人；每轮都要选择一个 bandit 动作。节点的收益取决于自己和直接子节点的动作，但每个节点只最大化自己的收益，所以局部理性一般不会自动最大化整棵树的社会福利。

作者证明，只允许父节点向子节点提供一次性、动作条件化的转移支付，就可以逐层对齐激励。它属于在线学习、算法契约/机制设计与多智能体博弈，而不是生信论文；联邦学习和分层数据收集只是动机示例。

### 模型

玩家位于深度 $D$、分支数 $B$ 的树上，共享大小为 $K$ 的动作集合。节点 $v$ 的随机奖励为

$$
X_t^v(a,a^{C(v)})=\theta^v(a,a^{C(v)})+z_t^v,
$$

其中均值未知且位于 $[0,1]$，噪声为零均值次高斯。父节点给子节点 $w$ 的激励是 $(B_t^w,\tau_t(w))$：只有子节点选择推荐动作 $B_t^w$ 时才得到付款。

### 方法流程

```text
子节点先学习到近似最佳响应
          ↓
父节点等待其 warm-up 结束
          ↓
对每个“子节点 × 推荐动作”做批量二分搜索
估计使该动作成为最佳响应的最小付款
          ↓
在估计上加入置信余量和强制响应余量
          ↓
把“自己的动作 + 给各子节点的推荐动作”视为组合臂
在扣除付款后的净奖励上运行 UCB/任意 no-regret bandit
          ↓
由叶到根递归传播
```

对动作 $b$，真实最小付款是子节点放弃自身最优动作的机会成本：

$$
\tau_b^\star(w)=\max_a\mu^{\star,w}(a)-\mu^{\star,w}(b).
$$

Exp Sub 从区间 $[0,1]$ 开始，每批 $\lceil T^\alpha\rceil$ 轮测试中点付款，通过接受次数判断阈值在哪一半。最终使用

$$
\widehat\tau_b(w)=\bar\tau_b^w+T^{-\beta}+CBT^{-\eta}.
$$

前一个余量覆盖估计误差，后一个余量把推荐动作变得足够有吸引力，减少子节点偏离以及由错误反馈造成的探索损失。随后节点在 $K^{B+1}$ 个组合臂上学习净奖励

$$
X_t^v(A_t^v,B_t^{C(v)})-\sum_{w\in C(v)}\widehat\tau_{B_t^w}(w).
$$

### 为什么要拆分 regret？

总 regret 被拆成动作 regret、付款过量和子节点偏离三部分。递归假设只传递动作 regret，从而减轻误差沿树向根部放大。采用随深度变化的参数后，深度 $d$ 节点得到

$$
\mathfrak R^v(T)=\widetilde{\mathcal O}\!\left(T^{1-1/(2d^2)}\right),
$$

整棵树的期望社会福利 regret 为 $o(T)$。深度越大，收敛越慢；宽度越大，$K^{B+1}$ 的组合臂数也越难计算。

### 实验与局限

实验只是一个深度 3、分支数 3、共 13 个玩家、每人 5 个动作的玩具模拟。图中越靠近根部 regret 越大，曲线低于线性参考，但没有误差带、重复次数、真实数据或系统性基线比较。

理论还依赖固定规则树、已知时间长度、平稳有界均值、可观察动作、次高斯噪声、短视且不会策略性操纵未来合同的玩家，以及较强的区间 regret 高概率条件。未找到官方代码或实验脚本。

## Online Decision-Making in Tree-Like Multi-Agent Games with Transfers

### Paper summary

This theoretical online-learning paper asks whether self-interested learners arranged in a principal-agent tree can be driven toward the action profile maximizing total reward, without a central coordinator. Each node chooses a bandit action, receives a reward depending only on its own and its children's actions, accepts a one-step action-contingent payment from its parent, and offers analogous payments to its children. Without transfers, local incentives can produce linear social-welfare loss. The paper shows that learned one-step transfers can align these local objectives and yield sublinear individual and aggregate regret.

The Jordan publication page uses the v1 title *Learning Contracts in Hierarchical Multi-Agent Systems* and algorithm name COBRA. The current arXiv v3 (October 2025) is titled *Online Decision-Making in Tree-Like Multi-Agent Games with Transfers* and renames the algorithm MAIL (Multi-Agent Incentivized Learning). It remains a 2025 arXiv preprint: no matching ICML 2025 PMLR record, journal reference, or arXiv conference comment was found.

### Method in brief

MAIL works recursively from well-learned children toward the root. For each node it (1) waits until each child satisfies a high-probability interval action-regret condition; (2) estimates, for every child-action pair, the smallest transfer that makes that action optimal using batched binary search (Exp Sub); and (3) runs a no-regret bandit routine such as UCB on a composite $K^{B+1}$-arm problem whose reward is the node's observed reward minus the estimated transfers. The estimated payment adds $1/T^\beta$ and $CBT^{-\eta}$ margins so it lies above the true threshold and makes deviations sufficiently unattractive.

The proof decomposes a node's regret into action, payment, and child-deviation terms. This separation prevents the full regret of descendants from recursively exploding upward. With depth-dependent parameters, a node at depth $d$ satisfies

$$
\mathfrak{R}^{v}(T)=\widetilde{\mathcal O}\!\left(T^{1-1/(2d^2)}\right),
$$

and the expected social-welfare regret is $o(T)$. Breadth enters computationally through the $K^{B+1}$ composite-arm bandit and payment terms, while depth worsens the exponent and the horizon requirement.

### Evidence and evaluation

The empirical evidence is illustrative rather than a benchmark: a depth-3, breadth-3 tree (13 players), five actions per player, and uniformly sampled reward means. Figure 2 shows sublinear-looking cumulative regret by depth and compares it with the theoretical dominant term and a linear reference. It does not report repeated-run uncertainty, quantitative tables, real data, or broad baseline comparisons.

### Reproducibility and limitations

- **Mode:** paper-only. No official code or experimental script was found in the paper, arXiv sidecars, author publication pages, exact-title/arXiv-ID searches, or GitHub search.
- **Strength:** complete pseudocode and detailed proofs make the theoretical construction inspectable.
- **Limits:** fixed regular tree and known horizon; stationary bounded local reward means; sub-Gaussian noise; observable child actions; myopic, non-strategically-manipulating expected-utility maximizers; child algorithms must satisfy the stated interval-regret concentration assumption; composite action space grows exponentially with breadth; regret degrades toward the root; evidence is only a toy simulation.
- **Domain:** online learning, algorithmic contract/incentive design, and multi-agent games. Federated/delegated learning is motivation, not the object empirically studied.
