## CONCH 方法解读

### 要解决的问题

病理人工智能常受限于标签稀缺，并且许多模型只看图像、只能服务于训练时定义的疾病或任务。CONCH（CONtrastive learning from Captions for Histopathology）把病理图像与生物医学文本放进同一表示空间，目标是在没有或只有少量下游标签时完成分类、检索、分割和图像描述（`paper.md:12`）。

### 核心思路

论文报告用超过 117 万个图像-说明文字对进行任务无关预训练（`paper.md:12`）。可见的扩展数据还比较了 CoCa 与 CLIP、仅人工筛选数据、仅 H&E 数据和未筛选数据；其中 human-only 设置为 1,170,647 对、H&E-only 设置为 457,372 对（`paper.md:354-359`）。

```text
病理 tile -> 图像编码器 -> 归一化图像向量 --+
                                           点积/相似度 -> 分类、检索或热图
类别名称与提示词 -> 分词器 -> 文本编码器 -> 归一化文本向量 --+
```

公开代码中的 `CoCa` 同时拥有视觉塔、文本塔和可选文本解码器（`coca_model.py:150-191`）。`encode_image` 与 `encode_text` 默认做 L2 归一化（`coca_model.py:207-227`），因此图文点积可作为相似度分数。

### 零样本分类如何工作

每个类别准备多个同义名称和多个模板。代码先为每个“名称-模板”组合编码，再平均并归一化，形成类别原型 $w_c$；图像向量 $v_i$ 的类别分数为 $s_{i,c}=v_i^\top w_c$（`zeroshot_path.py:12-42,160-164`）。论文的扩展数据直接比较了单提示词与提示词集成，在每个任务随机抽取 50 组提示词，结论是集成在多数任务中能提升表现（`paper.md:290-295`）。

全切片任务先得到每个 tile 的类别分数，再对每类最高的 $K$ 个 tile 分数取平均；这是 `topj_pooling` 的直接实现（`zeroshot_path.py:44-55`），也与论文说明的 top-$K$ pooling 一致（`paper.md:295`）。

### 证据与局限

主图展示零样本/监督分类、少样本切片分类、跨模态检索和零样本分割（`paper.md:63-80`）；扩展数据还包括少样本线性探测、EBRAINS 30 类脑肿瘤和图像描述结果（`paper.md:322-351`）。

但本地可获得的 Nature 导出是预览文本，没有完整 Methods；代码仓库是推理与下游使用发布，不含预训练数据构建、损失函数、优化器、训练脚本或完整权重。

## CONCH: A Visual-Language Foundation Model for Computational Pathology

**Paper:** Lu et al., *Nature Medicine* (2024), DOI `10.1038/s41591-024-02856-4`.

### Problem

Pathology models are commonly trained for one disease and one label space, making them expensive to build and difficult to transfer when annotated medical data are scarce. Most histopathology systems also learn only from images, despite pathology being communicated through detailed language about tissue structure, cell morphology, diagnosis, and uncertainty.

### Prior Limitations

- Conventional supervised whole-slide and ROI systems require task-specific labels and training pipelines.
- General CLIP, introduced at ICML 2021, learns from broad web image-text data rather than pathology-specific morphology and vocabulary (`paper.md:135`).
- Earlier biomedical/pathology vision-language systems include BiomedCLIP (arXiv 2023) and PLIP (*Nature Medicine*, 2023), but the CONCH figures show lower transfer performance on many pathology classification and retrieval tasks (`paper.md:149,159`; Figures 2, 4, and Extended Data Figure 2).
- Single-prompt zero-shot classification is unstable; Extended Data Figure 2 shows substantial variation across sampled prompts and motivates class-name/template ensembling.

### Proposed Method

CONCH (**CONtrastive learning from Captions for Histopathology**) is a task-agnostic visual-language foundation model trained from diverse histopathology images, biomedical text, and more than 1.17 million image-caption pairs (`paper.md:12`). The paper's schematic and ablations identify a CoCa-style design with an image encoder, text encoder, and multimodal decoder, combining a shared contrastive representation with caption-oriented learning (`figure_01.png`; `paper.md:354-359`).

At inference time, images and text are mapped into a normalized shared embedding space. The released code verifies three important transfer paths:

1. **Zero-shot classification:** average embeddings across class synonyms and prompt templates, then classify an image by image-prototype similarity.
2. **Whole-slide classification:** score pre-extracted tile embeddings and average the top $K$ tile logits per class (MI-Zero pooling).
3. **Cross-modal retrieval:** rank images or text using cosine similarity between normalized embeddings.

The paper also evaluates supervised/few-shot transfer, zero-shot segmentation, and caption generation, but those experiment pipelines are not included in the code snapshot.

### Evaluation and Main Findings

The abstract reports evaluation on 14 benchmarks covering classification, segmentation, captioning, and bidirectional retrieval (`paper.md:12`). The visible figures support the following conclusions:

- CONCH has the strongest displayed zero-shot classification performance across the main slide- and ROI-level comparisons against PLIP, BiomedCLIP, and OpenAI CLIP (Figure 2).
- Few-shot boxplots on TCGA BRCA, RCC, NSCLC, CRC100K, SICAP, and EBRAINS show strong label efficiency, especially with very few labeled examples (Figure 3; Extended Data Figures 6 and 7).
- Cross-modal retrieval mean recall is strongest in most displayed dataset/direction comparisons, and qualitative examples retrieve detailed morphology rather than only broad disease labels (Figure 4; Extended Data Figure 8).
- Similarity heatmaps align with pathologist-annotated tumor regions in renal, lung, and breast examples, while Figure 5 shows the highest displayed zero-shot segmentation bars (Figures 2 and 5; Extended Data Figures 3-5).
- Captioning improves METEOR and ROUGE over GIT baselines, but the paper explicitly notes imperfect outputs and occasional regurgitation from the small fine-tuning set (Extended Data Figure 9; `paper.md:346-351`).
- Ablations show that curation and initialization matter. CoCa is best on average for zero-shot classification, while a CLIP variant is best on average for retrieval; using all unfiltered pairs is not consistently superior to curated human-only data (Extended Data Figure 10; `paper.md:354-359`).

### Reproducibility

**Rating: 3/5 (inference workflows are documented; training and full experiments are not reproducible from this snapshot).**

Available and directly verified:

- CoCa model/config construction and deterministic image preprocessing.
- Custom 128-position tokenizer and normalized image/text embedding interfaces.
- Prompt-ensemble zero-shot classification for tiles/ROIs.
- MI-Zero top-$K$ pooling for pre-extracted WSI tile embeddings.
- Example notebooks for ROI classification, WSI classification, and image-text retrieval.

Major limitations:

- Model weights are gated externally and absent from the workspace. The repository states that public decoder weights were removed, so captioning cannot be reproduced with the released checkpoint (`README.md:116-146`).
- No pretraining code, data-curation implementation, losses, optimizer, scheduler, or training configuration is present.
- No supervised/few-shot training, segmentation, captioning, paper dataset preparation, or full benchmark runner is released.
- The acquired article is a subscription preview without full Methods/Results, and supplementary Markdown is unavailable.
- Notebooks contain placeholder paths and no stored outputs; datasets and precomputed WSI embeddings must be supplied separately.

Overall, CONCH provides strong evidence that pathology-specific image-language pretraining yields a broadly transferable representation. The released repository is useful for applying the encoder and understanding its inference interfaces, but it is not a complete reproduction package for the model's training or published benchmark suite.
