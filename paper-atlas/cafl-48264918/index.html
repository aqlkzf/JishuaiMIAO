Breaking Feedback Loops in Recommender Systems with Causal Inference

Open paper

CAFL：用因果推断切断推荐系统的反馈循环

1. 论文要解决什么问题？

在线推荐系统不是在一份静态、随机采样的数据上训练。它通常不断重复：

推荐物品 A_t
    ↓
只观察被消费物品的评分 R_t
    ↓
用累计数据拟合偏好参数 Θ̂_t
    ↓
Θ̂_t 决定下一轮推荐 A_{t+1}

于是模型会改变自己以后看到的数据。早期被推荐的物品更容易获得评分，这些评分又让类似物品继续被推荐。若直接把各轮数据合并、按普通独立样本训练，就会把推荐策略制造的选择偏差当成用户真实偏好。这可能降低评分预测和排序质量，也可能让不同用户看到的内容越来越相似。

论文提出 Causal Adjustment for Feedback Loops（CAFL）：不强迫平台随机推荐，而是在训练下一轮模型时，利用因果推断重新加权损失，使训练目标接近“外部强制指定推荐”时的干预分布。

2. 关键因果图

论文把多轮过程写成纵向结构因果模型：

真实偏好 Θ ─────────────────────────► 每一轮评分 R_t

A_1 → R_1 → Θ̂_1 → A_2 → R_2 → Θ̂_2 → ... → A_T → R_T
 └────────→ Θ̂_1    └────────→ Θ̂_2

$A_t$ 表示推荐矩阵，$R_t$ 表示观察到的评分，$\hat\Theta_t$ 是用历史推荐与评分拟合出的用户/物品参数。反馈路径经过上一轮模型状态 $\hat\Theta_{t-1}$：历史数据先改变模型预测，再由预测改变下一轮推荐。

这张图给出核心识别思路：若对 $A_t$ 做 $\operatorname{do}$ 干预，就切断 $\hat\Theta_{t-1}\to A_t$ 的箭头。各轮评分在干预分布下可以分解：

\[P(\{R_s\}_{s=1}^{t}\mid\operatorname{do}(\{A_s\}_{s=1}^{t})) =\prod_{s=1}^{t}P(R_s\mid\operatorname{do}(A_s)).\]

因此，真正想拟合的不是观察分布 $P(R_s\mid A_s)$，而是不会沿反馈路径选择样本的干预分布 $P(R_s\mid\operatorname{do}(A_s))$。

3. 在 positivity 成立时：按上一轮模型状态做 IPW

若给定 $\hat\Theta_{s-1}$ 后，每种推荐动作都有正概率，

\[P(A_s=a_s\mid\hat\Theta_{s-1})>0,\]

那么 $\hat\Theta_{s-1}$ 可以作为 backdoor adjustment 的调整变量：

\[P(R_s\mid\operatorname{do}(A_s=a_s)) =\int P(R_s\mid A_s=a_s,\hat\Theta_{s-1}) P(\hat\Theta_{s-1})\,d\hat\Theta_{s-1}.\]

相应的训练目标是加权对数似然：

\[\hat L_{\mathrm{IPW}}^{\mathrm{causal}}(\Theta) =\sum_{s,a_s} \frac{\mathbf 1\{A_s=a_s\}} {P(A_s=a_s\mid\hat\Theta_{s-1})} \log P_\Theta(R_s\mid A_s=a_s).\]

它与静态推荐中的普通曝光 IPW 有重要区别：分母不是只按人口统计变量或边际曝光率计算，而是使用生成该推荐时的模型状态和推荐策略。因为在这篇论文的因果图里，反馈正是通过 $\hat\Theta_{s-1}$ 进入下一轮推荐。

4. 最重要的扩展：positivity 被违反怎么办？

实际系统常规定“同一物品不能再次推荐给同一用户”。一旦某对 $(u,i)$ 已被推荐，它以后被推荐的概率就是零。普通 IPW 会除以零，不能使用。

CAFL 的办法不是凭空恢复这一轮的支持集，而是利用跨时间不变性：假设同一用户—物品对在不同轮次的干预评分分布保持不变。如果第 $s$ 轮某动作概率为零，就从其他概率为正的轮次借用同一 $(u,i)$ 和动作的似然贡献。

定理要求：

  1. 同一轮不同用户—物品对之间没有干扰，且模型似然能按 pair 分解；
  2. $P(R_{s,ui}\mid\operatorname{do}(A_{s,ui}=a))$ 随时间平稳；
  3. 每个 $(u,i)$ 至少在某一轮有非零推荐概率。

对概率为正的 cell，仍使用逆概率项；对概率为零的 cell，使用其他有支持轮次的经验平均。把两类项按 $c_s$ 组合（$\sum_s c_s=t$），就得到无偏的 CAFL 因果损失：

\[\hat\Theta_t^{\mathrm{causal}} =\arg\max_\Theta \hat L_{\mathrm{CAFL}}^{\mathrm{causal}}(\Theta;c).\]

在“一轮只采一个 pair、同一 pair 不重复”的特例中，它等价于给每条观测一个显式权重：

\[W_{ui}=\frac{UI}{UI-s} \left[(t-s)+\frac{UI-t}{UI-s+1} P(A_{s,ui}=a\mid\hat\Theta_{t-1})^{-1}\right].\]

这里逆概率项补偿推荐策略偏向，$t-s$ 补偿已经观察过的 pair 在后续轮次失去再次采样机会，剩余归一化项补偿可推荐集合随时间缩小。

5. 实际算法流程

输入：历史 {(A_s,R_s)}、各轮模型状态 Θ̂_{s-1}、
      日志策略概率 P(A_s|Θ̂_{s-1})、任意可加权的基础推荐损失
                         │
1. 为每条观测恢复当时的推荐概率
                         │
2. 概率 > 0：使用 inverse-probability likelihood
   概率 = 0：从其他有支持轮次借用同一 pair/action 的贡献
                         │
3. 用 c_s 或特例中的 W_ui 合并成 CAFL loss
                         │
4. 用加权最大似然/加权最小二乘重新拟合基础推荐器
                         │
输出：Θ̂_t^causal，再由原推荐策略产生 A_{t+1}

因此 CAFL 是一个“损失包装器”，不要求换掉矩阵分解、Poisson factorization、VAE 等基础架构。但平台必须知道或能够重建当时的推荐概率；仅凭评分数据无法推断一个未知黑盒策略。

6. 实验怎么验证？

主实验在 RecLab 的两个模拟环境上完成：

  • beta-rank-v1：来自 Chaney 等人的算法混杂环境；
  • ml-100k-v1：用 MovieLens 100K 拟合出的 100 维用户/物品隐向量初始化。

基础模型是交替最小二乘矩阵分解，CAFL 用加权最小二乘实现。每个实验重复十次。比较对象包括：

  • Feedback：未经校正、用自己推荐产生的数据训练；
  • Uniform：用均匀随机采样数据训练的“无反馈”参照；
  • 在另一组实验中还比较 Naive、Popularity、Poisson factorization 和 Pan et al. (2021)。

在随机保留的 100,000 个用户—物品 pair 上，CAFL 的 RMSE 和 NDCG 均优于 Feedback，但通常仍不如 Uniform。原因是 Uniform 真正看到更均匀、更丰富的数据，而 CAFL 只能用加权来近似其经验风险。

在 Pan 实验的 3,000 用户、1,000 物品模拟中，CAFL 报告 MSE $1.818\pm0.019$、MAE $1.015\pm0.005$；Pan 校正为 $1.896\pm0.042$、$1.042\pm0.011$。

7. homogenization 的结果不能过度解读

论文用不同用户推荐集合之间的 Jaccard 相似度衡量同质化：

  • 在 ml-100k-v1，Feedback 的确造成额外同质化，CAFL 能降低它；
  • 在 beta-rank-v1，CAFL 和 Uniform 反而比 Feedback 更同质化。

这说明 CAFL 优化的是因果校正后的预测风险，不是多样性目标。同质化可能来自模型归纳偏置、样本数量或数据结构，而不一定来自反馈循环。论文的 Figure 4 是一个重要的负面边界，而不是“CAFL 总能提高多样性”的证据。

8. 假设与可复现性边界

  • 若真实平台中还有未建模的路径，$\hat\Theta_{t-1}$ 可能不足以阻断 backdoor path。
  • 社交影响、库存竞争、生产者响应等会违反 no-interference。
  • 用其他轮次填补零概率 cell 依赖时间平稳性；用户偏好漂移会破坏保证，除非显式建立时间变化模型。
  • 极小推荐概率会导致高方差权重，即使理论 positivity 仍成立。
  • 论文只做模拟，没有真实平台干预或部署验证。
  • 未找到作者官方代码、实验脚本、环境快照和依赖锁定文件；因此本 workspace 为 paper-only，不能从作者快照独立重跑数值结果。

Breaking Feedback Loops in Recommender Systems with Causal Inference

Paper summary

Repeatedly retrained recommender systems create their own training distribution: recommendations determine which ratings are observed, those ratings update the preference model, and that model determines future recommendations. Pooling these adaptively collected observations as if they were independent can bias preference estimates, degrade prediction and ranking, and sometimes homogenize what users see.

Krauth, Wang, and Jordan introduce Causal Adjustment for Feedback Loops (CAFL). They model the full recommendation history as a longitudinal structural causal model and target the intervention distribution $P(R_t\mid\operatorname{do}(A_t))$ rather than the observational distribution $P(R_t\mid A_t)$. The graph implies that adjusting for the recommender’s prior fitted preference state $\hat\Theta_{t-1}$ is sufficient under the stated SCM. This converts a standard likelihood into a weighted likelihood whose inverse-probability term uses the probability of the recommendation under the actual policy and previous fitted state.

The important extension handles a common positivity failure: an item may be forbidden from being recommended to the same user twice. Ordinary inverse-probability weighting then divides by zero. CAFL uses stationarity of the user-item intervention response to borrow the corresponding likelihood contribution from other rounds where that action had positive probability. Under no within-round interference, time-stationary intervention distributions, and at least one supported round per user-item pair, the resulting loss remains unbiased for the feedback-free causal objective. In practice CAFL can wrap a loss-minimizing base recommender by changing observation weights rather than replacing its architecture.

Relationship to prior approaches

  • One-step missing-not-at-random/IPW methods condition exposure on user covariates or marginal exposure. CAFL conditions on the recommender’s previous prediction state because that state carries the multi-round feedback path.
  • Pan et al. (2021) require estimating marginal observation probabilities over time; the paper argues this is data-hungry and weak early in deployment.
  • Uniform random collection breaks feedback but sacrifices recommendation quality. The experiments use it as a feedback-free reference, not as the proposed deployed policy.
  • In the no-repeat, one-observation-per-step special case, CAFL coincides with the LURE active-learning risk estimator, while the causal derivation covers more general multi-observation/repeated-observation settings.

Evaluation and main findings

The main study uses two RecLab simulations: beta-rank-v1 and ml-100k-v1, the latter initialized from MovieLens 100K latent factors. Matrix factorization is trained by alternating/weighted least squares over ten repetitions and evaluated on a uniformly sampled 100,000-pair holdout. Against the uncorrected Feedback learner, CAFL improves both RMSE and NDCG in both environments, although the Uniform learner usually remains best because it observes more informative pairs.

Homogenization is evaluated by pairwise-user Jaccard similarity relative to an oracle. CAFL reduces excess homogenization in ml-100k-v1, where feedback causes the effect. It does not reduce homogenization in beta-rank-v1; there, CAFL and Uniform are more homogenizing than Feedback. This is a useful boundary: CAFL corrects the training distribution, not diversity directly, and homogenization can arise from model inductive bias or data volume rather than feedback.

In a separate 3,000-user/1,000-item simulation adapted from Pan et al., CAFL reports the best results among Naive, popularity, Poisson-factorization, Pan, and CAFL corrections: MSE $1.818\pm0.019$ and MAE $1.015\pm0.005$, compared with $1.896\pm0.042$ and $1.042\pm0.011$ for Pan’s method.

Assumptions and limitations

  • The identification result depends on the paper’s SCM and on $\hat\Theta_{t-1}$ blocking the relevant backdoor path. Unmodeled platform or user dynamics can violate this.
  • The positivity-violation theorem assumes no interference between user-item pairs and stationary intervention responses across time. Social influence, inventory competition, producer adaptation, or preference drift challenge these assumptions.
  • The logging/recommendation probabilities must be available. Very small probabilities can still produce unstable high-variance weights.
  • Evidence is entirely simulation based; there is no live-platform intervention, real longitudinal deployment, or robustness analysis under SCM misspecification.
  • CAFL does not guarantee lower homogenization.
